# Presto CLP Connector: Editorial Pitch and Reader Reception

## The 1-Pager Pitch

**Storyline**
- Presto gives every storage system the same SQL interface, and that interface says nothing about physical layout. For semi-structured data, the format still decides how changing field paths and recurring text are organized, compressed, and searched.
- The problem is ownership, not syntax. An analytical platform does not control the applications emitting logs, traces, audit records, or agent sessions. One JSON string per event preserves the variation and gives up field-level columnar execution. One global schema requires coordination the platform does not have.
- Variant enters as the lakehouse baseline, split into three layers: the engine supplies the SQL type and operators, Iceberg v3 defines the table type, Parquet defines the binary encoding and optional shredding. Shredding is presented as genuinely useful, with one condition attached: the writer selects the paths before the file opens, and adding them later means rewriting it.
- CLP answers the question that condition leaves open, which is what storage can do when the useful paths cannot be predicted. One archive holds many shape-specific encoded record tables behind a single logical event stream, an archive-local schema tree maps every observed path and type to physical columns, and logtypes store recurring static text once while writing extracted variables to typed columns.
- The advantage is scoped in the same section that states it. Uncontrolled upstream schemas and unpredictable query paths favor CLP. A stable schema gets the same field columns from ordinary typed columnar storage. Nearly unique shapes or nearly unique text leave CLP little repetition to exploit.
- The comparison then moves to physical work rather than capability. One table, five predicate shapes, starting after pushdown and asking only which data each format must scan, with the current open-source capability boundary marked inside the table instead of argued in prose.
- Work reduction is shown as three separately owned stages: ordinary metastore pruning, Uber's latest-`N` early termination with its watermark rule and its fallback, and archive-local CLP metadata narrowing the scan inside a selected split. Production figures arrive rounded and explicitly labeled as deployment observations rather than a benchmark.
- Operator experience is outcome first. One query combining typed exact paths, cross-field value search, and whole-event JSON reconstruction, then the short install path, the pluggable provider interfaces, and the read-only boundary.
- That short install path opens the final section. An out-of-band Presto Native connector needs a Java coordinator half and a dynamically loaded native worker half, two upstream PrestoDB hooks make that possible, and three lessons generalize the work: define the coordinator-to-worker contract, package one compatible unit, test the deployed boundary.
- The reader leaves able to make two decisions: which semi-structured storage belongs behind their Presto catalog for their own workload, and whether they can ship their own native connector out of tree instead of forking Presto to do it.

**Why we write this way**
- Variant is a measuring stick, not an opponent. It gets its own section, its real capabilities stated in the same sentences as its constraints, and the vocabulary it establishes is what the CLP section then answers.
- The comparison is deliberately deferred to physical work. Section 3 begins after pushdown, so the reader compares mechanisms rather than adjectives, and neither side is credited with an optimization the other also has.
- One table carries the entire comparison and the prose never restates it. Re-litigating the same point across three sections is exactly what turns a comparison into a rebuttal.
- Every CLP advantage ships with its own expiry condition in the same passage, not deferred to a caveat at the end. Section 2 closes on stable schemas and nearly unique shapes, and the conclusion states the rule once in both directions.
- Three evidence tiers are labeled in the running text, not in footnotes: current open source, Uber production extension, future direction. The Top-N optimization says outright that it is not in the open-source connector. The broader wildcard matching says it is being prepared for release.
- One reversal, used where it is earned. Top-N looks like it should bound the scan and does not, so the piece explains the conventional Hive-style boundary first, then Uber's cutoff, then the condition that makes the cutoff correct and the fallback for when it does not hold.
- Numbers are rounded and framed as what one deployment observes. The roughly 30x ratio arrives together with the reason it is not higher, which is high-cardinality trace IDs that compress poorly.
- History is corrected rather than sold. The earlier integration was native and lived in the Presto Native and Velox trees behind a fork, so the line is that native execution moved out of the fork, not out of the worker.
- Voice is third person throughout, with a single first-person plural in the opening used only to declare the article's method. The connector itself never speaks in "we," and the piece never addresses the reader as "you."

**Restrictions**
- Unshredded Variant is a native binary row-local value. Never textual JSON, never an opaque blob, never a row store, never literal row compression. Its scan cost is scoped to relevant `value` pages and per-row binary structure.
- No claim that Variant lacks pushdown, cannot express recursive predicates, or cannot preserve type-polymorphic values.
- The August 2025 conventional-Parquet benchmark may never be pointed at Variant or shredding. Those results predate the Variant stack. Any compression or query-performance ordering against Parquet Variant is a comparative claim requiring the same corpus, codec, shredding policy, query set, and reader versions, so it stays out of the piece entirely.
- "All fields shredded" is an analogy and must read as one. For changing shapes, comprehensive shredding still produces a file-wide sparse union. For stable schemas, the piece claims no field-columnization advantage at all.
- No sentence saying CLP has no nulls. Explicit source nulls are preserved, structurally absent fields simply are not present in an ERT, and Parquet encodes missing entries through definition levels rather than null payloads.
- The availability asymmetry always carries its time qualifier. CLP works in PrestoDB through the external connector, and as of Q3 2026 PrestoDB does not yet support Variant end to end.
- Every advantage names the workload condition that creates it and the condition under which it fades. No "superior," no "best," no universal comparative.
- The earlier implementation is never called an external plugin and never called non-native. It was native execution in Velox workers of a Presto fork.
- Current open-source search capability is cross-field typed value search. Broader arbitrary wildcard key and path matching exists in deployment and is being prepared for release, and nothing may imply that a general wildcard-projection API is already public.
- Uber's metastore, latest-`N` early termination, and billion-file scale are production extensions rather than open source, and Uber's metastore is never presented as the reference provider. Pruning and early termination stay distinct in every sentence that touches either.
- Rounded production observations only: tens of petabytes per month with qualitative headroom, billions of file records, roughly 30x on enriched logs, a handful of workers. No exact ingest volume, compressed volume, core count, latency, or throughput, and never framed as a controlled comparison with another format.
- Compressed KV-IR files, which are appendable, streamable, and searchable, stay distinct from immutable archives. ERT and archive-local schema-tree mechanics describe the archive path only.
- Portability is always qualified by libc, architecture, packaged dependencies, Presto Native and Velox pins, and target CPU flags. Folly's `F14LinkCheck` makes CPU-intrinsics mode part of the loading contract, not a build detail.
- Testing is divided by runtime dependency. Native helpers free of Presto and Velox symbols may be unit-tested, and plugin-bound code whose symbols come from the worker requires a real worker.
- Three information aids total: the predicate table, the one query, the one diagram. No benchmark dashboard, second layout diagram, full configuration block, optimizer walkthrough, or API inventory.
- No Pinot analogy and no Uber hot-tier architecture. Neither advances the connector thesis.
- YScope, IBM and Presto-community, and Uber attribution is preserved wherever production evidence or upstream infrastructure appears.
- One superlative is permitted and ships unhedged: the first end-to-end plugin across the Presto Native connector boundary. The chronology check is complete, so "one of the first" is not restored.

**Tone**
- Refuses to be a Variant rebuttal. Shredding keeps its real benefits, compression and projection and statistics and skipping, stated in the article's own voice, and the piece never scores a point Variant would concede anyway.
- Refuses to be a connector advertisement. Read-only access, the `default` schema boundary, the wildcard capability that is not public yet, and the stable-schema case where CLP has no columnization advantage all sit in the reader-facing text rather than in a footnote.
- Refuses to persuade with numbers. Rounded observations from one deployment, with an explicit statement that they are not a benchmark against another format.
- Refuses a struggle narrative. Every section describes a working system, and the only history present exists to correct the record about what the earlier integration actually was.
- Refuses to sell the future. Future PrestoDB Variant support is described as something that would improve CLP's SQL surface without changing its physical storage, which is a smaller and more checkable claim than a roadmap.
- The reason is the audience. PrestoDB maintainers, connector authors, and lakehouse engineers can open the two linked PRs, the connector repository, the Compose stacks, the Parquet and Iceberg specifications, and the μSlope paper. One overclaim a reader can falsify in a browser tab discounts every other claim in the piece, including the upstream contribution the article wants the community to reuse.
- The second reason is the ask. The article invites other authors to treat this connector as a reference pattern, and that invitation only lands from a document that reads like engineering documentation rather than a launch post.

**YScope's angle**
- The Presto community holds the people who would build the next out-of-band native connector. A reusable pattern published to them earns standing that a product post cannot, and it reaches an audience Uber's own blog never will.
- The durable asset in this piece is the boundary, not the connector. Two upstream PrestoDB hooks, a public repository, a documented packaging flow, and an integration-test stack are all things other authors will use, with IBM and Presto-community review named beside them.
- Placing CLP next to Variant on physical grounds makes CLP a comparison point inside the lakehouse conversation rather than an outsider asking for a seat. The article never claims that position, it simply conducts the comparison at that level and lets the reader place it.
- Restraint is the credential. Naming the workloads where CLP has no advantage is precisely what makes the workloads where it does have one believable to readers who can check.
- Uber supplies the scale evidence, but the reader being recruited is anyone with uncontrolled schemas at petabyte scale. The Uber name carries the numbers, and the open-source connector carries the invitation.
- The complementarity framing protects the project's future. When PrestoDB gains Variant, CLP's SQL surface can converge while its physical layout stays its own, so the connector never reads as a stopgap waiting to be obsoleted.
- This post is also where the sibling Uber article defers its connector internals, so publishing it closes YScope's own citation loop across both pieces.

## Pros, Weaknesses, Strategy

**Pros**

- The hierarchy the contract demands actually holds in the word counts. Variant gets 117 words out of 2,674, roughly four percent, and the comparison is confined to one table and one conclusion paragraph. The piece cannot be read as a Variant rebuttal because it never spends enough of itself arguing with Variant to become one.
- Every advantage names the condition that creates it and the condition under which it fades, and it does so structurally rather than through hedging adjectives. Section 2 closes on stable schemas and near-unique shapes, the section 3 table marks its own capability boundary, and the conclusion repeats the fade condition in its second sentence. Three independent places, no superlatives anywhere.
- Evidence labels are applied where a skeptical reader looks first. Uber's latest-`N` planning is called a production extension and explicitly excluded from the open-source connector, and the 30x figure carries its disclaimer in the same paragraph rather than in a footnote.
- The SQL example is defensively correct in the two ways that matter. It uses a one-sided `LIKE 'timeout%'`, which is what the public tests actually cover, and it immediately corrects its own function name: `CLP_WILDCARD_STRING_COLUMN()` performs cross-field value search "despite its name." Volunteering that a shipped function name overclaims is the single most credibility-buying sentence in the draft.
- Section 6 is genuinely reusable by someone who does not care about CLP. It names the handle inventory, the two upstream hooks, the packaging constraints, and the testing split, and it points at seven real files. This is the material that earns the piece a place on a community blog instead of a vendor blog.
- The connector history is stated precisely and identically in both places it appears, closing the ambiguity the safeguards care about most. "Native execution moved out of the fork, not out of the worker" prevents any reading in which the earlier integration was non-native or a plugin.
- Voice is clean and consistent: zero em dashes, zero second person, and exactly one "we," in the opening. Total length is 2,674 against a 2,500 to 2,700 target, and every section sits inside its budget band.

**Weaknesses**

- Section 2 breaks a Non-Negotiable Safeguard outright. The draft says CLP avoids "one giant table containing every possible column, with nulls wherever a field is absent," which is exactly the literal-null framing the contract forbids, and it omits the required counterweight that Parquet encodes missing entries through definition levels rather than null payloads. A Parquet-literate reader knows sparse columns are cheap, and this is the one paragraph where the piece argues against a straw man.
- The article's most impressive mechanism is neither open source nor a property of CLP. Section 4 is the largest section at 578 words, 22 percent of the piece, and its centerpiece is Uber's latest-`N` cutoff. The draft labels the availability boundary honestly but never concedes the portability one: Iceberg manifests also carry per-file bounds and record counts, so the same cutoff is implementable behind an Iceberg connector. A hostile reader concludes the piece borrowed a metastore achievement to flatter a storage format.
- The same section overstates what a reader can actually deploy. It says the connector scans both storage forms "when its split provider supplies them," but never states what the section contract requires, that the shipped MySQL provider currently emits archive splits. Someone who follows the documented setup gets less than the sentence implies, and the omission points in the flattering direction.
- The section 3 table compares unlike things in two ways. Three of five rows hedge Variant with "unless another index covers the search" without naming what exists, meaning Parquet column statistics, page indexes, bloom filters, and Iceberg file bounds, while the CLP column names its mechanisms concretely. Row four then sets Variant's shipped behavior against a CLP capability "being prepared for open-source release." The label is honest, but a table row is a comparison, and shipped versus unshipped inside one is the cell a Variant advocate screenshots.
- The convenience schema is bolded, defined in one clause, and dropped. The draft cut the clause that makes it interesting and non-promotional, that no archive is rewritten because the ERT columns already exist. As written it reads as a feature name rather than a consequence of the storage model, which is the advertisement failure mode in miniature.
- The 30x figure's subject does not match its source. The knowledge base sources 29 to 33x to the whole onboarded workload, 25 PB raw against 750 to 850 TB compressed, but the draft attributes it to "the enriched log records" within Uber's mix. That reads as a subset measurement, which is a different and unsupported claim.
- Nothing on the Verify Before Publication list is closed, and the draft states all of it in present tense as shipped fact: the merge state and behavior of PRs #27653 and #27652, the exact spelling of `use-connector-provided-serialization-codecs`, the release matrix including `manylinux_2_28` and the `F14LinkCheck` mode, whether the docs URL describes the pluggable connector or the historical in-tree one, both Compose files, and `task integration-tests:run`. Nine links point into `main` branches that will drift after publication.
- Link balance is the countable thing that makes the advertisement charge available regardless of tone. Fourteen of 24 link instances point to YScope-owned properties against four to PrestoDB issues, PRs, and docs.
- The "first end-to-end plugin across that boundary" claim is internally verified but externally uncheckable, and it appears on the community's own blog where the reflex is to discount a superlative on sight. Its only mitigation is adjacency to the two openable PRs, which the draft does provide.
- Publication mechanics are unfinished. The diagram is referenced as a relative `images/` path, is not in the Presto site repo, and uses light-only hex fills with no dark-mode handling. Separately, the sibling Uber post is blocked on this post's URL through two `PRESTO_BLOG_URL` placeholders, so this draft now sits on another team's critical path while carrying no reciprocal link itself.
- Two small consistency items. The draft has one prose semicolon in the opening and one inside a table cell, both of which the sibling piece forbids, and the acknowledgments run 107 words against a 70 to 90 budget in a piece with only 26 words of total headroom. Also, "AI coding-agent sessions" in the opening is the only claim in the draft with no backing anywhere in the knowledge base.

**Strategy**

- Fix the null sentence first. Add the definition-levels clause the safeguard requires, and drop "with nulls wherever a field is absent." One clause converts the piece's weakest paragraph into evidence that the author knows Parquet well enough to be fair to it, which is worth more than the sentence it replaces.
- Add one conceding sentence to section 4 saying the latest-`N` cutoff depends on trustworthy per-file bounds and matching counts, not on CLP, and that any connector whose metastore supplies them could apply it. Then let the existing ownership line do its job. Conceding this costs nothing the piece was actually claiming and removes the strongest available charge against the largest section.
- State the shipped-provider limitation plainly, that the MySQL reference provider emits archive splits today. An availability gap disclosed by the author is a credential. The same gap found by a reader is a retraction.
- Restore the convenience-schema clause about ERT columns already existing, and correct the 30x attribution to the onboarded workload mix rather than the enriched subset. Both are one-line edits, and the acknowledgments overage funds all three additions inside budget.
- Run the Verify list as a single mechanical pass against one connector release, not as a set of separate questions: PR merge state, config-flag spelling, release matrix, docs URL, both Compose links, and the integration-test task. Pin the nine `main`-branch links to a release tag or commit SHA in the same pass so the article does not rot three months after publication.
- Commit the diagram to the Presto site repo, rewrite the path, and either make the fills theme-safe or set it on an explicit neutral background so it does not invert badly. Trim acknowledgments to budget and remove both semicolons for house consistency with the sibling piece.
- Do not add a benchmark or any CLP-versus-Variant number to close the section 3 asymmetry. That asymmetry is uncomfortable precisely because the honest alternative does not exist yet, meaning a controlled comparison over the same corpus, codec, shredding policy, query set, and reader versions. Publishing an ordering the piece cannot defend is the one failure that would retroactively turn every other hedge in the article into noise. Name the boundary, keep the numbers out, and accept the discomfort.
- Do not expand the "unless another index covers" hedges into a fair-comparison paragraph. Enumerating Parquet page indexes and bloom filters would push section 3 past budget and turn the table into the Variant tutorial the contract puts Out of Scope. The hedge is deliberately generous and should stay generous rather than become exhaustive.
- Do not re-hedge the "first" claim back to "one of the first." The chronology is verified, both articles now state it identically, and retreating would contradict the sibling and read as a walk-back. Its mitigation is placement, not wording, and the placement is already correct at one sentence from two links a skeptic can open.
- Do not add Trino. Naming a competing engine's shipped Variant on PrestoDB's own blog invites exactly the vendor-comparison frame the piece spends 2,674 words avoiding, and the time-qualified PrestoDB statement is already the accurate one. The knowledge base's guardrail is to scope the claim, not to draw the comparison.
- Do not shrink section 4 to reduce the Uber-production share of the piece. The three-stage story is what makes the article useful to operators rather than to connector authors only, and the labels already carry the availability boundary. The correct fix is the one conceding sentence above, not a smaller section.
- Do not touch the single "we" or the coding-agent example. Rewriting the opening around one first-person plural costs words the budget does not have, and the acknowledgments already identify who "we" is. The coding-agent mention is two words in a list of four examples, and developing it would import the sibling article's closing move into a piece whose contract has no room for it.
- Do not add the reciprocal link to the Uber post before it publishes. This post ships first by design. Treat the cross-link as a post-publication edit, and send the live URL to the Uber piece's owner the day this goes out, since two placeholders there are blocked on it.
